# Electric bus scheduling under electricity tariffs and shared chargers

**Research and solver review · 2 November 2026**

The next solver experiment should measure **time to a replayed, exactly-once dispatch at a fixed fleet size**. In the October paired Partille capacity campaign, C2 at eight buses had a certified weighted event-network LP with fractional route weight 8.0 under both depot-power settings. Each reported pool selected nine buses. C3 and C6 reached eight under both settings. This makes C2 a useful historical challenge and C3 a positive control. It does not establish that eight vehicles are feasible or infeasible under either experimental model. Some previously reported target matches used covering masters that served passenger trips more than once.

Two changes target different failures. To improve the first integer schedule, select compatible passenger-trip sequences and retime their charging together. To improve the time to a pricing certificate, price a smaller candidate graph first and retain a complete pricing pass on the true duals. Time the two separately before combining them. An electricity-cost claim additionally needs an incumbent from the same physical model on fixed duties and a comparable fleet count.

### Solver engines

**Gurobi.** Its API reuses LP basis information when a compatible model is modified and provides MIP starts. Our driver already keeps a model open, adds sparse columns and forces dual simplex. Profile LP time before tuning. In a fixed-dual snapshot compare automatic/primal/dual simplex where relevant. Adding a variable normally preserves primal feasibility, while adding a violated row favors dual reoptimization. Gurobi explicitly requires an external branch tree for custom branching and node-wise variable generation. An OPTIMAL result for a final MIP on saved columns proves the modeled pool problem to tolerance. It cannot certify undiscovered duties. [Warm-start guidance](https://doc.gurobi.com/projects/optimizer/en/current/features/warmstart.html) · [Effect of model changes](https://support.gurobi.com/hc/en-us/articles/19809472289169-Does-Gurobi-use-warmstarts-after-changing-a-previously-optimized-model) · [Branch-and-price limits](https://support.gurobi.com/hc/en-us/articles/360013421051-How-do-I-modify-the-branch-and-bound-algorithm-or-create-a-branch-price-and-cut-algorithm)

**SCIP / GCG.** SCIP exposes reduced-cost pricing for a feasible node relaxation and Farkas pricing to recover or establish infeasibility of an infeasible one. Its Ryan–Foster example filters columns under local branch decisions. GCG accepts specialized pricing solvers and distinguishes heuristic and exact solve callbacks. A trial adapter must pass all master duals, branch restrictions, charge/occupancy descriptors and rigorous pricing status, including at infeasible restricted nodes. Compare to enumerated integer optima on toys, with forced together/apart branches. Incremental changes to row sets, disabled columns and certificates are the integration risk. [SCIP pricer contract](https://www.scipopt.org/doc/html/PRICER.php) · [SCIP branching example](https://www.scipopt.org/doc-3.2.0/examples/Binpacking/BRANCHING.php) · [GCG pricing-solver API](https://scipopt.github.io/PyGCGOpt/api/pricingsolver.html)

**VRPSolver / BaPCod.** The developers’ framework implements branch-cut-and-price, leverages resource-constrained paths, packing-set-based cuts and branching, and supports route enumeration after reduced-cost fixing. VRPSolverEasy exposes a narrower routing interface. The project distributes compiled libraries and the full academic interface has its own usage terms. Do not infer support for arbitrary shared station-minute rows, time-varying charge arcs and end-energy duals from ordinary time-window support. First map one tiny instance to the interface and compare feasible route attributes, LP and IP with enumeration. If the needed row coefficient cannot be carried into pricing, compare only the explicitly simplified common model. [Framework paper](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/7238.pdf) · [BaPCod manual](https://bapcod.math.u-bordeaux.fr/BaPCodUserGuide0.74.pdf) · [VRPSolver terms](https://vrpsolver.math.u-bordeaux.fr/start/getting-started/) · [VRPSolverEasy interface](https://github.com/inria-UFF/VRPSolverEasy/blob/master/README.rst)

**A shorter integer proof when a small gap is already documented.** On a finite model with a fixed fleet and matching objective, suppose a replayed incumbent costs U and a globally feasible dual has objective D. Every route has nonnegative reduced cost. By weak duality and the nonnegative contribution of slack, any improving integral solution can use only routes with reduced cost at most U − D, allowing a conservative numerical margin. Enumerate **every** route in that band (or a proved equivalent representative for every relevant coefficient vector), then solve the resulting MIP. This can close the integer gap without maintaining a price-and-branch tree. The existing batch limit and its cheapest placement for one state transition cannot certify that enumeration because alternatives with distinct plug-minute footprints can serve different integer combinations. Bound the time and memory of a pilot and flag incomplete enumeration explicitly. A raw restricted-master dual or a capped list of near-best paths does not meet the premise. [Pessoa et al., §3.3, complete path enumeration and its conditions](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/7238.pdf)

Do not spend the first pilot adding generic rank-1 cuts, a trained arc classifier or a framework dependency. The current DAG has no cut-parity state. A valid cut in the master changes pricing for every unseen route. Optimize those extensions only after a violated cut or a measured bottleneck is available. A taper model should also use verified energy increments from the **starting SOC**, not assume an under-approximation of full-from-empty charge implies conservative increments at intermediate SOC. [Nafstad et al., pricing and cut resources](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/trsc.2024.0725) · [Löbel, Borndörfer & Weider, charge-increment analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14446)

## Matched benchmark and reporting

1. **Freeze information and inputs.** Hash trips, complete directed deadhead times/energy, tariff intervals including the billing horizon, station map/power/plug inventory, vehicle and terminal rules, start costs, fleet objective, master sense and graph restrictions. For the GIRO-facing Partille scenario use PARX 60 kW and explicitly state the separate plug assumption and each opportunity station’s rate. Minute occupancy needs consistent boundaries. Verify continuous start/end event overlap under the intended rule. State whether overnight or final charging is allowed and billed. The recorded operational schedule and any incumbent-derived templates are off-limits to a blind construction except as an explicitly labelled seeded arm.
2. **Start small.** Test a tiny independently enumerated case with one congested charger, a price boundary and a positive charging-start fee. Include a feasible plan with exactly back-to-back charges and an infeasible overlap. Compare *all* objective components, signed minimum reduced costs and exact-trip coverage. Next compare a fixed list of five-bus cases, including the flat-tariff control and a depot/time-sensitive case. Evaluate the historical C2 k8 challenge with C3 k8 as positive control. If a more recent baseline supersedes these, select using its frozen identifiers and state the change. Test held-out duty subsets/tariffs before generalizing beyond one timetable.
3. **Run both practical and algorithmic controls.** The six arms are (a) the recorded operational schedule replayed when physically feasible, (b) charging reoptimized on those trip duties, (c) blocking without GIRO assignments or tariff information followed by the same charging optimizer and ex-post billing, (d) a blind joint baseline using the full pricer with a saved-pool MIP and optional de Vos-style dive, (e) the candidate method, and (f) a price-aware local search using the same checker. On tiny data add an independent direct MIP/enumerator. For each arm record which routes, charge sites, visit sequences, charging duration and start times are adjustable. A newly scheduled duty with saved trip assignments belongs in the fixed-assignment family.
4. **Control budgets and report missing outcomes.** Match hardware, threads, allowed time, memory, tariff, objective sequence, stopping tolerances and initialization. Pair deterministic seeds across arms. For stochastic pilots use a prespecified small list (e.g., five), and show per-instance outcomes. Include cold graph build, data conversion, retiming, physical replay and certification in wall time. Also report warm-reuse time and one-time preparation. Separate time to first valid target from time to certificate. Record timeouts, no-incumbent and proved-infeasible counts out of all attempts. Conditional median cost among successful runs complements these counts. A Gurobi WorkLimit controls solver work on the same hardware/settings. 6. **Keep distinct result columns.** For each run, record scheduler exit, CG stop and domain, column and row counts, and the best valid global pricing lower estimate. Separately record the final restricted LP value and fractional route weight, any fleet-only bound, the finite-pool MIP incumbent and bound, the independently replayed dispatch and cost, and attainment of the fleet target. In minimization an uncertified restricted master value can exceed the full LP optimum. With dual-feasible signs, a certified global lower bound on reduced cost and a proven route-mass cap, a Lagrangian correction can yield a scoped global LP lower bound. A route found by a heuristic supplies an *upper* bound on the minimum reduced cost. Never substitute it for the lower estimate.

At common fleet and scope, let **F** be a feasible cost after optimizing charging on the frozen duties, **U** the best feasible joint cost, and **L** a valid lower bound for the joint problem. A demonstrated saving is **F − U**. The largest possible saving relative to the specific feasible benchmark **F** is bounded by **F − L**. If comparing *optimal* fixed and joint costs and the fixed-duty solve is incomplete, retain its separate lower bound as well. A negative F − U generally records an unfinished search when the duty plan is admissible in the joint model. Per-station peak kW, peak-window kWh, cost, deadhead time, charge starts and per-bus ending SOC distinguish mechanisms that average electricity cost conflates. Improvements under a lower terminal-energy obligation or a more powerful charger cannot establish a routing benefit.

## Evidence limits

This is a targeted primary-source review of fixed-timetable EV scheduling, its algorithmic neighbors, and publicly documented commercial methods. Search terms covered capacitated electric-bus CG, tariff-aware charging, candidate graph selection, dynamic discretization, exact pricing, IPColGen, branch-price-and-check, HASTUS/MinBus/CrewOpt, IVU and Optibus. Published papers, authors’ manuscripts, official software manuals and first-party vendor descriptions supply the links. The October 2026 GERAD contribution is a working paper. The JOPT contribution is a conference abstract. A disclosed method in an industrial collaboration, deployment of an earlier product, product features and results reproduced on our data are four different evidence types. No vendor was run or contacted for this review, and no new acceleration, capacity inventory or optimal fleet is claimed.
