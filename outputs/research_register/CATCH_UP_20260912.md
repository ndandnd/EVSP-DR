# EVSP DR catch up on 12 September

**We have stronger small-instance results, promising charging-cost results, and an unfinished larger-instance method. Shared charger capacity is the main unresolved modeling extension.** This summary uses the verified 15:34 EDT results and a later live queue check on 12 September. A chain is a nested sequence of trip sets: increasing k adds one GIRO duty's trips.

| Question | Verified position | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Can we match small GIRO fleets? | With set covering and full-pool inheritance, all six chains match k=3, 4, 5 and 6. At k=2, five chains use two buses; chain 5 uses three. | We have a credible small-instance baseline, with one unresolved k=2 exception. |
| Can we match ten buses? | Chains 3, 5 and 6 reach ten at k=10. | Yes on these instances; the other full-pool chains were interrupted, so this is not a six-chain success claim. Chain 3 also received fresh-solver routes. |
| What happens beyond ten? | The limited-import extension reaches 11 buses at k=11 in chain 6. At k=15, completed chains 3, 4, 5 and 6 use 17, 18, 17 and 18 buses. | We can obtain solutions at this size, but do not consistently match GIRO or prove the best possible fleet. |
| Do stricter charger constraints work? | Slower PARX charging alone matched the small pilot targets. Adding shared charger capacity gave 3 buses for a 2-bus target and 16 for a 3-bus target, without a CG certificate. | Search ran out of time; this does not show that those extra buses are necessary. These pilots are separate from the six chains and do not impose a 65% return-SOC floor. |
| Does decomposition help? | Splitting 32 GIRO duties into four groups produced saved constructions using 35–37 buses. | A useful first construction. Attempts to improve the recombined problem timed out during startup; shared charger capacity is not certified. |

**Why things improved.** We now retain useful routes from smaller instances. On the same chain, an independent k=8 run needed nine buses in its saved pool, while inheritance supplied enough useful routes to use eight. This is evidence that the available route pool matters. Set covering is also used, so the improvement over the earlier tables should not all be attributed to warm starts.

**Why we still miss targets.** There are three separate delays:

1. **Preparation:** checking every inherited route sometimes consumed the whole run. The extension checks at most 512 routes for 15 minutes, then begins CG. That changes the starting pool and is recorded as a separate treatment. A worker-shutdown bug also prevented one deadline from taking effect; its corrected k14 retry has now completed CG.
2. **Route generation:** with shared charging limits, one k=3 search took 7.15 hours while its LP solve took 0.006 seconds. Here the missing step is completing pricing.
3. **Integer selection:** some runs finish CG but still lack an integer solution matching GIRO. When the MIP proves a higher minimum within the saved pool, reaching the target requires different columns. When the MIP has an unresolved gap, we cannot yet separate missing columns from incomplete integer search.

CG certification concerns the fractional problem on the represented graph and tolerance. A final MIP proof concerns only the routes supplied to it. Neither statement alone proves full-model integer optimality. Baseline individual-route replay passed, but duplicate-trip removal and shared-capacity feasibility need their own checks.

**Charging costs remain a useful paper direction.** The five-bus comparison keeps a common aggregate return-energy minimum. For the morning-price-peak example, modeled charging plus start fees are approximately 490 for the original GIRO charging schedule repriced, 276 after optimizing charging on the same trip sequences, and 258 after allowing different trip combinations from the augmented route pool. This is a 240-kWh / 350-kW pilot without shared charger limits, not yet an operator-savings claim. The fixed-duty method uses DP to generate charging alternatives and a small integer model to select one per duty. The joint method uses the saved CG columns plus those fixed-duty alternatives.

**My priority order.** Complete the affected k14/k15 follow-ons; resolve the k=2 exception and cases with proved pool gaps; then make the small capacity-constrained pricing tests finish. Expand decomposition only after fixing its startup bottleneck. These give more useful answers than launching more copies of runs already known to stall.

**Current operations.** Unicorn is accessible. The latest queue check shows three running jobs: chain 1 k15 CG, corrected chain 2 k15 CG, and chain 2 k14 MIP. The chain 2 k15 MIP waits for its CG result. Many older queued jobs are blocked by failed prerequisites rather than waiting for available CPUs. Among 139 completed earlier default-partition MIPs, none has a confirmed preemption; this supports using default for these one-hour solves but does not guarantee future reliability.
