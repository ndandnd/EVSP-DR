# Fleet comparison figures

Generated by [`generate_fleet_comparisons.py`](generate_fleet_comparisons.py) from the saved experiment register and checked warm-chain status tables. No solver was rerun for these figures.

## Figure 1: fresh partition versus fresh covering

- PNG: [`fresh_partition_vs_cover_fleet_matrix.png`](fresh_partition_vs_cover_fleet_matrix.png)
- PDF: [`fresh_partition_vs_cover_fleet_matrix.pdf`](fresh_partition_vs_cover_fleet_matrix.pdf)
- Data: [`fresh_fleet_matrix.csv`](fresh_fleet_matrix.csv)

The left panel uses the current six-chain partitioning MIP records from `nested84`. The right panel uses the completed fresh covering records for chains 1, 3, and 5 from `covering_rerun9`. The covering cells for chains 2, 4, and 6 are grey because their CG results are not yet available; they were submitted as job 810454 and should remain missing until the output passes the result checks.

Cell text is the selected fleet followed by a proof code:

- `OPT`: full frozen-pool lexicographic MIP optimum.
- `F`: the fleet stage was proved, while the subsequent charging-cost stage was time-limited.
- `~`: an incumbent exists, but the fleet stage was not proved.

Color is based on the actual selected fleet minus target `k`. It is a visual severity scale, not a new bound. Every result is a finite-pool MIP result; the CG certificate and independent overlap lower bound are separate evidence and are not encoded by the cell color.

The partition and covering panels are workflow comparisons. The different master row sense changes the duals and therefore changes which routes enter the pool. They are not a MIP-only comparison over one identical frozen pool.

## Figure 2: fresh versus previous-k inherited covering

- PNG: [`fresh_vs_inherited_covering_chain3.png`](fresh_vs_inherited_covering_chain3.png)
- PDF: [`fresh_vs_inherited_covering_chain3.pdf`](fresh_vs_inherited_covering_chain3.pdf)
- Data: [`fresh_vs_warm_cover_chain3.csv`](fresh_vs_warm_cover_chain3.csv)

This figure compares fresh covering and previous-k inherited covering on chain 3 at k=5, 8, and 10. The input SHA-256 is checked equal between the fresh and warm rows for each k. The fresh arm starts from singleton routes; the warm arm imports the prior-k route pool and adds the current singleton routes.

The upper panel separates measured wall time:

- Fresh blue bar: total fresh CG wall time.
- Warm gold segment: inherited-pool import time reported in `cg_status.csv`.
- Warm light-blue segment: `warm total - warm import`, computed for display. This is a residual, not a separately instrumented phase.

Measured values are:

| k | Fresh total (min) | Warm total (min) | Warm import (min) | Warm after import (min) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | 8.4 | 32.4 | 25.9 | 6.5 |
| 8 | 22.2 | 137.1 | 133.2 | 3.9 |
| 10 | 48.9 | 292.6 | 282.6 | 10.0 |

The lower panel shows the finite-pool MIP fleet for each arm against the target line. The inherited route pool reaches 8 buses at k=8 and 10 buses at k=10 in the saved results, while the fresh covering pools report 9 and 11 respectively; these are pool-specific outcomes, not a proof that warm inheritance is globally better. The same `OPT`, `F`, and `~` proof codes are used as in Figure 1.
