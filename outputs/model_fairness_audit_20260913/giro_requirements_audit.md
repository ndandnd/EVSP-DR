# GIRO requirements audit from original attachments

**Audit date:** 2026-09-13
**Purpose:** establish which operational constraints are actually supported by the supplied GIRO evidence and whether the current EVSP-DR comparisons use a fair physical model.

## Finding

The original attachments contain substantially more physical and operational information than the route-dictionary dump alone: group-specific nominal and usable battery energy, SOC-dependent opportunity-charging curves, depot power, charger counts, SOC floors and targets, layover/turnaround rules, interlining restrictions, and time-dependent deadhead data.

The central interpretation is:

- **15% is the hard general SOC minimum.** It is stated in the PDFs as “no vehicle below 15% SOC at any point” and appears as `Min 15.00%` for a `Recharge` activity in the Notes screenshots.
- **Partille 65% and Frölunda 60% are recharge-activity targets, not documented terminal/end-of-duty hard floors.** The exact Notes characteristic is `Vta state of charge at end`, with `Activity=Recharge`, `Frequency=when equal to`, `Min`, `Target`, and `Max`. The original workbooks contradict treating the target as a terminal floor: all 42 literal Partille tasks end below 65%; 57 of 61 Frölunda tasks end below 60% (derived from the original `Data` sheets). A target may have an objective or scheduling interpretation, but the attachments do not say which.
- **60 kW is only the listed depot rate** (`PARX` in Partille, `KEX` in Frölunda). Opportunity chargers use the SOC-dependent curves below and reach 371.5 kW or 386 kW at low SOC. A constant 240 or 350 kW is therefore a declared abstraction, not GIRO physics.
- The current fee comparisons are treatment-isolated within their chosen abstraction, but they are **not fair GIRO operational comparisons** until group-specific capacity/rates, SOC floors, shared stations, and the applicable stop/interlining rules are represented or explicitly scoped out.

## Original workbook cross-checks

The workbook values are useful evidence of what the supplied schedule actually did, but they do not by themselves turn every behavior into a hard rule.

- Partille `Data!A1:X2205` has 2,204 activity rows, 42 literal task IDs, 987 regular rows, 621 deadhead rows, 352 recharge rows, and 61 rows each for prep/pull activities. The first `SOC before` (`Data` column T) is 100.000000% for all 28 `18E2` task IDs and 99.9999924% for all 14 `18E1` task IDs (floating-point rounding); this is the recorded schedule's starting state, not an explicit universal initial-SOC rule. The recharge scaling is consistent with 236.44 kWh for route-21 `18E1` blocks and 239.01 kWh for local `18E2` blocks. Representative original rows are `Data row 10` (`7880C`, 6:23–6:34, SOC 66.367→83.250), `row 18` (`PARX`, 7:59–10:00, 54.065→100), `row 34` (`JON_A`, 14:15–14:25, 45.640→64.879), `row 38` (`3127L`, 15:09–15:18, 52.114→68.864), `row 1568` (`4808`, 5:19–5:25, 70.508→81.071), and `row 1578` (`2190L`, 9:35–9:40, 41.877→52.791). These are examples, not a claim that one row is the full station specification.
- Partille source station occupancy, computed from positive-duration recharge windows `[start,end)`, peaks at 1/1/2/1/1 on 2190L/4808/3127L/7880C/JON_A for each mechanically admissible 40-duty variant pairing, and 11 at PARX. This fits the documented opportunity counts. The 42 literal tasks include day-variant siblings; the attachment does not provide a formal suffix-to-weekday legend, so no exact day pairing should be claimed from the names alone.
- Frölunda `Data!A1:X2560` has 2,559 activity rows, 61 tasks, 1,393 regular rows, 523 deadhead rows, and 311 recharge rows. Six tasks exceed 20 h (`11302`, `11306`, `11308`, `11312`, `11313`, `11322`); their recorded minimum SOC is 25.211–51.544%, consistent with the special 20% rule. Examples are `Data row 6` (`1179`, 5:46–5:52), `row 19` (`KEX`, 9:03–11:46), `row 27` (`4760L`, 13:52–14:11), `row 43` (`KB_B`, 18:17–18:21), and `row 2140` (`4290L`, 5:47–5:56, `18E3` scaling).
- Frölunda source occupancy peaks at 1 for 1179/2350/3680/4290L, 2 for 2530L and 4760L, and 20 at KEX across all 61 tasks. The 4760L result is only a connected-window count and assumes the physical two-port interpretation; the repeated group rows require confirmation of pooling.
- A recharge row's `kWh × 60 / minutes` is an average over a rounded schedule window. It does not establish a constant nameplate draw or a sub-minute plug trace. The source gives no feeder/site demand ceiling.

## Confirmed, inferred, and unknown

**Confirmed directly by an original attachment:** battery nominal/efficiency inputs; PDF usable capacities; discharge/idle values; SOC curve rows; 60 kW depot rows; opportunity counts; 15% floor; FDL 20% rule; weekday scope/effective dates; no-shift statements; route-specific interlining, successor, layover, and block-link restrictions; and DHD interval tables.

**Derived or inferred for modeling:** usable capacities are the effective energy scales used by the recorded SOC/recharge rows; `0.03` is read as 3 minutes because the source schedule uses h.mm duration strings; `4290`/`4290L` and `3680`/`KB_B` are aliases; positive recharge windows are treated as occupied ports for the overlap audit. These conventions should be recorded with each experiment.

**Unknown from the supplied originals:** raw email body/export; formal weekday suffix legend for Partille variants; whether charging power is grid-side or battery-side; curve interpolation/step semantics; exact setup/minimum for FDL `18E3` remote rows and KEX; whether repeated FDL group capacity rows pool at 4760L; sub-minute plug/unplug trace; site feeder/demand ceiling; complete driver-hours/break/relief rules; and tariff rates or demand-charge parameters.

**Deliberately out of scope here:** reconstructing legal crew feasibility, inventing missing demand tariffs, or treating a recharge target as a terminal hard constraint. The audit concerns the operations-model inputs needed for fair GIRO physical comparisons.
